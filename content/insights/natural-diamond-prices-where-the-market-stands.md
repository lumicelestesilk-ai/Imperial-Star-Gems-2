After a sharp run-up in the post-pandemic years, natural diamond prices spent a long stretch moving the other way. The fall hasn't been even, though, and the detail matters more to a buyer than the headline figure. This note looks at where prices sit, why the market has split, and how to approach a purchase now.

## Why the market has split

Several pressures have acted at once, and they don't hit every stone equally.

**Demand cooled after the peak.** The post-pandemic surge in jewellery spending faded, and key consumer markets bought less.

**Lab-grown competes hardest at the lower end.** Where a buyer chooses mainly on size for budget, a grown stone is an easy substitute. That pressure is much weaker on the rare, large and high-grade natural goods that grown stones don't replace.

**Producers have held back supply.** Miners and the midstream have slowed rough sales to support prices, which cushions the market but also shows how much stock is waiting.

## What it means if you are buying

> The rarer the stone, the less its price has behaved like the headline.

1. **Compare like with like.** A quoted fall in "diamond prices" usually describes the broad middle of the market, not the specific stone you are looking at.
2. **Grade and cut still set value.** Two stones of the same weight can sit far apart on price. The report and the cut matter as much as the carat.
3. **Natural and grown are different purchases.** Natural goods are finite, and the finer ones have held value better. Grown goods are a manufactured product priced as one. [Our note on lab-grown pricing](/insights/lab-grown-prices-what-the-decline-means-for-buyers) covers the other side.
