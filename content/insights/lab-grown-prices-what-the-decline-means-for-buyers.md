Few prices in the diamond trade have moved as far, or as fast, as lab-grown. A one-carat grown stone that commanded a real premium a few years ago now trades at a small fraction of that figure, and wholesale prices have kept drifting lower. This note sets out why, and what it changes for anyone buying grown goods, whether for a single piece or for stock.

## Where prices sit now

As of this month, a well-cut one-carat round lab-grown diamond around G colour and VS clarity trades at roughly TK per carat at wholesale, against about TK a year ago. At two carats the figure is TK per carat. Fancy shapes are TK.

The gap between wholesale and retail is still wide. Retail prices have fallen too, but more slowly, which is why the same stone can be quoted at very different figures depending on where you ask.

## Why prices keep falling

Grown diamonds are a manufactured product, and they behave like one.

- **Capacity has grown quickly.** Growers, particularly in India and China, have added reactors and improved yields, so more carats come out of each growth run.
- **Costs have come down.** Faster and more reliable growth cuts the cost of every stone, and competition passes most of that saving on.
- **Supply isn't limited by nature.** A mine can only give up what the ground holds. A grower can make more whenever the price covers the cost, so there is little to hold prices up once supply catches up with demand.

None of this is a flaw in the product. A grown diamond is still a diamond. It simply has the price behaviour of a manufactured good rather than a scarce one.

## What it means if you are buying

> Buy a grown stone for what it is today, not for what you hope it will be worth later.

**For a single stone,** the falling price is mostly good news: the same budget buys more size or a better grade than it would have a year ago. The trade-off is resale. Expect little or no secondary market value, and don't treat a grown diamond as a store of value.

**For trade buyers holding stock,** the priorities change:

1. **Keep stock turning.** Stones held for months are likely to be repriced below what you paid.
2. **Quote on the day.** A price list printed last quarter is probably already too high.
3. **Buy on grade and cut, not on carat alone.** As prices fall, well-cut, well-graded stones hold their appeal better than stones that are merely large.
4. **Insist on a report.** Every grown stone we sell carries an independent grading report stating its origin. [Browse lab-grown stock](/lab-grown-diamonds) or ask us for today's figures.
